- 2026-09-09 · ADR-0014 proposed, nothing implemented · The operator's decomposition of the `CANDLES` freshness bound checked out on two of three legs and got more interesting on the third. Verified in code: execution price at swap time is bounded by a **live** Jupiter quote at `maxQuoteAgeMs` 15 s enforced as `QUOTE_STALE`, and the position monitor's executable mark is `exitQuote` at the full quantity — neither reads candles, so the 90 s candle bound protects neither. But three findings cut the other way and are recorded rather than buried: **90 s is probably derived, not arbitrary** (the momentum trigger's shortest window is `minReturn15m`, and 15 min ÷ 10 = 90 s, a conventional tolerance that simply was never written down as one); the decomposition **misses a fourth consumer**, `highSince`, which feeds the trailing high-water mark from candles and where a stale candle *understates* the high and therefore sets the trailing stop **lower** — degradation in the loosening direction; and the class-level health row **cannot express the thing that matters**, because `ops.provider_health` is one row per `(provider, dataClass)` and after the WP1b narrowing it will read HEALTHY on a newest-across-the-set basis while an individual asset is an hour stale, with **no per-asset input-age check anywhere in the entry path**. So the ADR proposes a shape and deliberately refuses to pick the number: split the requirement by consumer, derive `SIGNAL_INPUT` in the open between 90 s and `candidateTtlMs` (10 min) without choosing, add the per-asset check that does not exist and let it BLOCK, and keep every existing BLOCK. It explicitly does not propose 45 minutes — the cost-motivated answer — because `maxCandidateAgeMs` bounds how long a candidate may *sit*, a different quantity from how old its inputs were when it was made. Also added to the review #1 brief: **fixing the cause and leaving the detector alone** as a sub-pattern with four unaudited candidates, since DEFECT-4 was recorded as a symptom on 2026-09-08, its planner cause fixed, and the health row that had just demonstrated it left to go on lying for another day. · `docs/decisions/ADR-0014-candle-freshness-per-consumer.md` (Proposed, operator decision required), `docs/reviews/review-1-brief.md`.

- 2026-09-09 · WP1b · **DEFECT-4: `HEALTHY` meant "the call succeeded", not "the data is current".** Found by answering the operator's question — does the existing freshness gate already refuse stale-input candidates, in which case cold features mean fewer decisions rather than wrong ones? It does not, and it cannot: `evaluateFreshness` computed age as `now − lastSuccessAt`, the recency of our last provider *call*, never the age of the newest *datum*. The worker calls OHLCV every cycle and the call succeeds even when it returns nothing new (78% of the time), so `BIRDEYE:CANDLES` reported HEALTHY with `freshness_age_ms: 2306` while the newest 1m candle for all 43 eligible assets was 232–347 minutes old — and because `effectOnEntries` is only set when the state is not HEALTHY, ADR-0011's `BLOCK` for candles could never fire at any staleness. The 2026-09-08 M4 entry recorded this exact symptom and fixed the planner starvation beneath it; nobody fixed the alarm, so it was still reporting HEALTHY a day later. The feed is now only as fresh as the worse of call recency and datum age, an empty store is FAILED, and market-ingest supplies the newest 1m bucket including what the cycle just wrote. Proved against the old evaluator: `expected 'HEALTHY' to be 'FAILED'`. Also landed, per the operator's decision: candle tracking narrowed to held + ELIGIBLE + reference series (eligibility reads token_overview/token_security, never candles, so nothing needs candle history to *become* eligible), eviction after five consecutive empty fetches so a dead token stops costing four requests a day forever, and `ops.provider_spend_daily` written in the same statement as the monthly row — the absence of a daily series is what made the run-rate error possible and cost WP1 a day of excavation. The tier stays on Lite: the founded requirement is ADR-0011's 90 s / 5 min for candles, not `maxCandidateAgeMs`, and REST polling meets it for about one asset on Lite and five at the degraded bound, so the gap is architectural (WebSocket starts at Premium) rather than a $60 purchase. · `docs/probes/data-budget-2026-09-09.md` (correction + DEFECT-4), `libs/market/src/freshness/evaluate.{ts,spec.ts}`, `market-ingest.ts`, `market-repo.ts`, `supabase/migrations/20260909004400_provider_spend_daily.sql`; 20 projects green.

- 2026-09-09 · WP1 · The data budget measured rather than estimated, and the premise it started from corrected. **We are not leaving 83% of the allowance unspent — we are spending 71.6% of it.** That premise came from my own arithmetic error: 129,514 CU divided by 9 elapsed calendar days instead of the 2.171 days ingestion actually ran, which turned a 59,656 CU/day run rate into a fictitious 14,390. - 2026-09-09 · M3/MP · Turnkey `TradingWalletSigner` (D47, D51, D55, §15.7, §15.7A; INV-25, INV-26 mapped) — the reference production signer exists, key-free and testable; only the live probes remain operator work. **Stamping** (`turnkey-stamp.ts`) implements the provider's API-key scheme on `node:crypto` rather than adding an SDK to the executor bundle: the raw JSON body signed with ECDSA P-256, DER then hex, `{publicKey, scheme, signature}` base64url into `X-Stamp`. The compressed public key is decompressed with a single modular square root (P-256's prime is 3 mod 4) so the private scalar becomes a JWK with no elliptic-curve dependency, and the constructor **refuses a mismatched key pair** by signing and verifying a probe before the process serves anything. **The adapter** (`turnkey.ts`) sends only bytes that already passed authorization, structural validation, simulation and chase/expiry, and carries three refusals in front of the provider rather than behind it: the message must hash to what the request claims (§15.4, so a timeout retry presents the same hash and gets the cached signature without a second provider call); the transaction must pass the local mirror of the pinned policy, so a shape the provider would deny never leaves the host and never lands in its audit log as an attempted violation (INV-25); and the whole signed transaction that comes back is re-decoded and refused unless its message bytes are **identical** to what was submitted. A `CONSENSUS_NEEDED` activity is a failure, not a wait — an autonomous principal that needs human approval to sign an already-authorized transaction is a policy misconfiguration.
